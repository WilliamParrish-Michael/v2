Standards & documentation

What we test, and what travels with every lot.

A research buyer's confidence comes from the paperwork as much as the material. Here's how we characterize each lot and what you'll find on its certificate of analysis.

01 — The certificate of analysis

One document that describes exactly what a lot is.

A certificate of analysis (COA) is the record of the analytical tests run on a specific lot of material. It ties a batch to its results: what was measured, by which method, and what the method found.

Every lot we release is accompanied by its own COA. Because results are lot-specific, the document reflects that batch — not a generic template — which is what makes it useful for recordkeeping and reproducibility on the buyer's side.

03 — Reading your COA

How to check a lot against its paperwork.

  • 01

    Match the lot number

    Confirm the lot number on the COA matches the label on the container you received. The document describes that batch specifically.

  • 02

    Check identity first

    Compare the observed molecular weight from mass spectrometry against the theoretical value. They should agree within the method's tolerance.

  • 03

    Read purity in context

    Note the HPLC purity figure and the method conditions. Purity is only meaningful alongside the method that produced it.

  • 04

    Account for net content

    Use net peptide content, water, and counterion figures when precise material amounts matter for your records.

  • 05

    File it with the material

    Keep the COA with your lot records so results stay traceable to the batch throughout its use in your facility.

04 — Storage & handling

General handling for lyophilized material.

These are standard laboratory practices for handling lyophilized peptide powder. Specific conditions should follow your own facility's protocols.

Store cold

Keep lyophilized material frozen and desiccated, protected from light and moisture, for stability during storage.

Warm before opening

Allow a sealed container to reach room temperature before opening to reduce condensation on cold material.

Limit cycles

Minimize repeated freeze–thaw cycles, which can affect material over time. Aliquot where appropriate.

05 — Traceability

Every batch stays identifiable.

Each lot carries a unique identifier that links the physical material to its COA and our production records. That chain — lot number, documentation, and batch record — is what lets a result be traced back to a specific batch if a question comes up later.

Questions about a lot or its documentation? Contact us and reference the lot number on your container.
